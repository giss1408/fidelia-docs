## Findings: what investors will look for and not find

Fidelia is well thought out but was written for regulators and analysts, not investors: it explains carefully what Fidelia will not do, and shows little evidence that anyone wants it. Investors in a crowd back three things: proof of demand, a team they trust, and a believable way to get their money back. The brief was weakest on exactly those three.

## The story, rewritten

**One sentence.** Fidelia helps neighbourhood shops in Abidjan bring their customers back, and turns their everyday sales into proof a lender can trust.

**One merchant** *(illustrative; replace with a real pilot merchant and her own words after Phase 0)*. Awa runs a maquis in Yopougon. Forty customers a day, about 4 million francs a month, paid in cash, Wave and Orange Money. At night she adds up three wallets and a cash box by hand. She knows her regulars by face, but nothing brings them back on purpose. When she asks a microfinance institution for 1 million francs to buy stock, she has nothing to show: no records, no history, no proof.

**The problem.** Côte d'Ivoire has more than 25 million mobile-money accounts, but the strict banking rate is only about 31% (BCEAO, 2023). Paying is solved. Proving that a small business is real and stable is not, so millions of shops like Awa's cannot get working capital.

**What Fidelia does.**

1. **Day 1, a cash book.** Every sale, cash or mobile money, in one daily total. No more adding up wallets at night.
2. **Week 1, customers who come back.** Customers earn points with their phone number, no card and no app required. Awa sees each week how many came back and how many new customers the Fidelia app brought her.
3. **Month 3 and after, proof.** Every sale builds a history Awa owns. With her consent, she can show it to a licensed lender.

**Why merchants pay.** If loyalty brings back 5% more sales, a small maquis earns about 73,000 F more gross profit a month, against a subscription of about 5,000–7,500 F (illustrative, to confirm in the pilot).

**Why now.** Merchant payment points jumped from 1.13 million to 2.72 million in a year. Wave made accepting payments almost free, so the next value is what sits on top: recognising customers and proving activity. Fidelia plugs into the wallet the merchant already uses, at no extra fee.

**Why us.** *(To write: what each founder has built or sold before, and why they will win in Abidjan.)*

**The ask.** XOF 31 million (about €47,000) to run a six-month free pilot with 5 to 10 maquis and grocery shops in one commune, and prove three numbers: merchants record most of their sales, the app brings them new customers, and at least 40% choose to pay when the pilot ends.

**What we will never do.** Hold your money, lend it, or sell your data. Licensed banks and MFIs keep that role.

## Market sizing

Fidelia's reachable market is about **120,000 maquis and grocery outlets in Abidjan, worth about XOF 10.8 billion a year** in subscriptions. The three-year goal of 1,800 paying merchants is 1.5% of it. These are top-down estimates: no reliable count of maquis and grocery shops in Abidjan exists yet ([MARKET.md](MARKET.md) says so), so each assumption is named and must be checked.

## Competitor table

The real competitor is the paper notebook; the biggest threat is a wallet operator adding loyalty. No tool found today combines a cash book across wallets, loyalty by phone number and a history a lender accepts.

| Alternative | What the merchant gets | What is missing | Threat | Fidelia's answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Paper notebook and loyalty cards** (status quo) | Free, familiar, no phone needed | No totals across wallets, no customer recognition, no proof for a lender | High: it is what Fidelia must replace | Day-1 cash book faster than the notebook; loyalty without cards |
| **Wallet merchant apps** (Wave, Orange Money, MTN MoMo) | Payments and a transaction list, one operator each; Wave charges ~1% | No loyalty or retention tools found; each covers one wallet, none covers cash | **High if they add loyalty** | Works across every wallet plus cash; operator-neutral; partner rather than competitor |
| **WhatsApp Business and Facebook** | Free promotion to existing contacts | No sales records, no measure of who came back; customers ignore broadcasts | Medium | Offers by push in the customer app, measured at the counter ("Client venu") |
| **Point-of-sale apps with loyalty** (e.g. Loyverse) | Sales recording, stock, a loyalty module | Not connected to mobile money; no consented history for lenders; local adoption unknown | Medium | Wallet capture, offline-first on cheap phones, proof for credit |
| **Payment aggregators** (CinetPay) | One integration for several wallets | ~3% + 50 F per payment, higher than Wave; no loyalty | Low | Fidelia never reroutes payments, so it never costs more |
| **SME fintechs** (Julaya, Hub2) | B2B payments and treasury | Built for larger firms, not a maquis | Low today | Different segment; possible partners |
| **Merchant platforms elsewhere in Africa** (Moniepoint, Nigeria) | Payments, then credit from payment data | Not known to operate in Côte d'Ivoire | Medium long term | Local presence and MFI partnerships first; their success shows the model works |
